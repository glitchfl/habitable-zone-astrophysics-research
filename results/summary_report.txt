2. MODEL AND SCENARIOS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Tp(a) = Teff * ((1-A)/(4 eps))^(1/4) * (R*/a)^(1/2)
  a(T)  = R*   * ((1-A)/(4 eps))^(1/2) * (Teff/T)^2
  thresholds  T_hot = 373.15 K   T_cold = 273.15 K
  reference   A = 0.30   eps = 0.60  (gives Earth 289.3 K at 1 AU)

5. DOES SENSITIVITY DEPEND ON THE STAR
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Substituting R* = sqrt(L/(4 pi sigma Teff^4)) into a(T) cancels Teff exactly:
      a(T) = sqrt(L/(4 pi sigma)) * sqrt((1-A)/(4 eps)) / T^2
  verified numerically to 6.7e-16 relative.

  RELATIVE sensitivity: identical for all 2670 stars.
    star-to-star spread of da/a_ref, worst scenario: 1.22e-15  (machine precision)
    -> no dependence on Teff, on L, or on spectral class. none.

  ABSOLUTE sensitivity: depends on the star, through sqrt(L) alone.
    log-log slope of a_in against L = 0.500000000000  (exact 1/2, max resid 8.9e-16)
    median |da_in| for the strong-greenhouse scenario, by class:
      F  0.4421 AU   (9.5x the M-dwarf value)
      G  0.2819 AU   (6.0x the M-dwarf value)
      K  0.1332 AU   (2.9x the M-dwarf value)
      M  0.0467 AU   (1.0x the M-dwarf value)

  The apparent Teff trend is luminosity in disguise:
    corr(a_in, Teff) over the sample      +0.8861      <- looks like a real trend
    corr(log a_in, log L)                 +1.00000000
    std of log a_in after removing log L   2.42e-16   <- nothing left for Teff
    std of Teff after removing log L       287.0 K      <- yet Teff varies freely
    8 real Gaia stars within 1% in L span 607 K in Teff,
      and their a_in agree to 0.93% - the 1% L spread, not the Teff spread.
    control: L fixed at 1 Lsun, Teff 3200/5772/7400 K -> a_in identical to 2.2e-16 AU

6. WHAT THIS MEANS, AND WHERE IT STOPS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * A and eps move both boundaries by one common factor sqrt((1-A)/(4 eps)).
    Over the scenario set that factor runs from -35% to +60% of the reference.
  * eps is the stronger lever: dropping eps 0.60 -> 0.30 gives +41.4%, while
    raising A 0.30 -> 0.50 gives only -15.5%.
  * Answer to the research question: the sensitivity of the boundaries to A and eps
    does NOT depend on the type of star in relative terms. In absolute terms a bright
    F star's boundary moves ~9x further than an M dwarf's for the same atmosphere
    change, but that factor is sqrt(L_F/L_M), not a Teff effect.
  * Limits: this is a grey 0-D model. Because A and eps are taken as constants that
    do not depend on wavelength, the star's spectrum cannot influence the result, and
    the Teff cancellation above is a direct consequence of that choice. A real M dwarf
    planet has a lower albedo against red light and different H2O/CO2 absorption, so a
    spectrally resolved model WOULD produce a Teff dependence. The clean null result
    here is a statement about the model, and that is what makes it a useful baseline.
